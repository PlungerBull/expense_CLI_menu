Why enter=cancel and not enter=dead (v1 proposed dead): a dead key makes the modal look hung, and PromptModal→ConfirmModal chains (Auth main-currency) would flip enter's meaning mid-flow — a double-enter user stalls silently. Enter-as-safe-default has real precedent (k9s default-focuses Cancel; mutt takes enter as the bracketed default), keeps one-keystroke dismissal, and is self-revealing: the list is unchanged, the item is still there. This is the TUI analog of the flat CLI's required --yes on reconcile revert.

Why d/b/t stay
Habits form on the daily screens (d=Delete on Inbox); the slip onto Rates opens a harmless prompt, and the reverse slip opens a confirm that dies on enter/n/esc. Unique non-mnemonic letters would trade three learnable collisions for six unlearnable keys. Longer-term direction (not blocking): Rates is the only screen that uses letter-jumps to set form values — every other multi-field surface uses the bar-cycle form idiom. Converting Rates to a three-field form dissolves d/b/t at the root; write it down so no fourth letter-jump screen gets added.
Implementation traps (from the mechanics review)
1. Recon-detail r=refresh: _load_txns and the write worker share the default exclusive worker group — give the reload its own group= or a refresh mid-POST cancels the wrapper while the thread still lands the write. 2. The reload must re-fetch the reconciliation record (header balances + read-only gate), not just the checklist. 3. Keymap text lives in four places that drift: BINDINGS (footer), hand-written hint Statics, module/class docstrings, and mockups — update all, and add a test pinning hint ↔ binding agreement.
