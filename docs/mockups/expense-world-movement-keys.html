§4 · The delete test — the only question on this page

For each family: if it vanished tomorrow, what becomes unreachable? That splits the five families cleanly in two, and it is the whole basis for going ultra-simplistic.

Free to delete — nothing becomes unreachable

j  k  h  l  ,  .

Six keys, zero capability. Every one is a second name for an arrow or a page key that is already bound, already in the footer, and already works.

What you gain: j k h l become free as command letters on every list screen. Today they are unusable — bind l to anything and the list widget silently eats it first. That is four letters back for the shortcuts you said you want to invent next.

What you lose: vim muscle memory, for whoever has it. Nothing else. They are already invisible — j k were dropped from the footer on 2026-08-20 and h l never printed their letter.

Cannot delete without a replacement

pgdn  pgup  [  ]

Four keys, and each is the only door to something.

pgdn / pgup — delete these and page 2 of Transactions, Inbox, Activity and Rates is unreachable, because the arrows clamp at the edge on purpose (§3). Any "arrows only" plan has to either reopen that 2026-07-13 decision or give paging a different home.

[ and ] — delete these and the Monthly report is frozen on the last four months. They are the strangest key in the app: one screen, one job, and the reason they exist at all is that ←→ were already spent on expand/collapse in that same grid. If you want them gone, the window needs another axis — shift+←→ is free and would read as "move the container, not the cursor".

The one-sentence summary: of the ten keys you asked about, six are duplicates you can delete for free, and four exist only because the arrow keys ran out — twice at the page edge, once inside the month grid.

Nothing here has been changed in code. This page is the inventory you asked for, so the next call is yours: delete the six duplicates, and/or decide where paging and the month window live if the arrows are to be the only movement keys.

§5 · Decided so far — and the one key still open

2026-08-27. Deleting: j k h l , . — six duplicates, zero capability lost. Keeping: pgdn pgup — load-bearing, no arrow reaches page 2. Still open: [ ], the one-screen key.

The finding that makes [ ] easy to remove: on all three tree views, expand/collapse already has a complete second door — enter and space both toggle the row (reports.py:72 · outstanding.py:122, 202). So ← → on a tree is a duplicate in exactly the same way h l is. Freeing it costs nothing. The only question is what should take the month window.
A · ← → becomes the month window
┌─ Monthly report ─────────────┐ │ MAY JUN JUL AUG │ │ ▼ Food -380 -412 -395 -401│ │ #dinner -90 -110 -88 -102│ │ ▶ Rent -900 -900 -900 -900│ └──────────────────────────────┘ esc Back r Refresh ⏎ Expand ← Older → Newer ┌─ Outstanding ────────────────┐ │ ▼ Food -412.00 │ │ ├ Groceries -280.00 │ └──────────────────────────────┘ esc Back r Refresh ⏎ Expand/collapse ← → do nothing
One key, one meaning: ←→ = move along the data's horizontal axis, everywhere. Expand/collapse drops to ⏎/space.
The risk: that grid looks like a tree — rows carry ▼/▶. A hand that presses → on a ▶ row expects it to open, and instead the whole window slides. Same gesture, very different result.
B · ⇧← ⇧→ takes the window
┌─ Monthly report ─────────────┐ │ MAY JUN JUL AUG │ │ ▼ Food -380 -412 -395 -401│ │ #dinner -90 -110 -88 -102│ │ ▶ Rent -900 -900 -900 -900│ └──────────────────────────────┘ esc Back r Refresh → Expand ← Collapse ⇧← Older ⇧→ Newer ┌─ Outstanding ────────────────┐ │ ▼ Food -412.00 │ │ ├ Groceries -280.00 │ └──────────────────────────────┘ esc Back r Refresh → Expand ← Collapse (unchanged)
Nothing retrained. ←→ stays expand/collapse everywhere; the window moves one modifier up — "shift = move the container, not the cursor".
The cost: a third tier of arrow keys, and ⇧← is not a key anyone discovers by accident.
C · pgdn pgup takes the window
┌─ Monthly report ─────────────┐ │ MAY JUN JUL AUG │ │ ▼ Food -380 -412 -395 -401│ │ #dinner -90 -110 -88 -102│ │ ▶ Rent -900 -900 -900 -900│ └──────────────────────────────┘ esc Back r Refresh → Expand ← Collapse pgdn Older pgup Newer ┌─ Outstanding ────────────────┐ │ ▼ Food -412.00 │ │ ├ Groceries -280.00 │ └──────────────────────────────┘ esc Back r Refresh → Expand ← Collapse (unchanged)
No new key family at all. pgdn/pgup are already staying, and both are completely unbound on the report today (verified: reports.py has no page bindings).
And it unifies a meaning: pgdn/pgup = "the next window of data" — 20 more rows on a list, 4 more months on the report. ←→ untouched, [ ] gone, total key families down by one.
C looks like the answer. It is the only one of the three that removes [ ] without adding anything: no retraining of ←→, no new modifier tier, and one honest meaning for a key family you had already decided to keep. The single open detail is direction — pgdn = older reads right if the ledger lists newest-first (scrolling down = further back in time), but say the word and it flips.

§6 · Before deleting , and . — a keyboard question

On a MacBook there is no physical PgDn or PgUp key. They are fn+↓ and fn+↑ — a two-key chord. Which means , and . are not really duplicates on that keyboard: they are the only single-key way to page, and deleting them makes paging harder, not simpler, on the ten screens that page.

This flips depending on hardware, so it is a question, not a finding: do you drive this on the laptop keyboard, or on an external one with real PgDn/PgUp keys? If it is the laptop, the ultra-simplistic move is arguably the opposite of what it looks like — keep , . as the primary page keys and let pgdn/pgup be the alias.
