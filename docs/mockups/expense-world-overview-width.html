Overview — packing the columns left

The ask, 2026-08-29, verbatim: “If you see this image, youll see that data is going too much to the right, id liek it more tightly coupled even if there is space.”

Nothing is built. Pick one and it gets built; that is the rule in CLAUDE.md (“Mock every screen before building it”) and showing is not approval.

Provenance. Every figure on this page is real — read off your live Overview on 2026-08-29 (5 accounts, 10 categories, flow 2026-05 → 2026-08). The frames are character-exact: generated, not hand-spaced, so the gaps you see are the gaps the terminal draws. They are drawn at 110 columns so they stay readable here; your real terminal is roughly 200, where every gap below is about twice as wide.

1 · Why it happens

Two lines of overview.py, one cause. The grid is Table(expand=True) with add_column("Category", ratio=1): expand makes the table fill the pane and ratio=1 hands all the slack to the label column, so on a 200-column terminal roughly 140 blank columns get stuffed between your category names and your numbers. The Accounts panel is the same table call without the ratio, which pads the name column to the same effect. Nothing is aligning to the right on purpose — the numbers are right-aligned inside columns that were themselves inflated.

The content actually needs 59 columns: 15 for the widest label (▶ SUSCRIPCIONES) and 44 for four numeric columns at the widest cell (467,189.17). Everything past 59 is padding.

2 · Option A — natural width

expand=False on both tables. Each table becomes exactly as wide as its content and sits at the left margin. The tightest possible answer, and the smallest diff — two keyword changes.
The cost: the cursor bar shrinks to the content too, so the reverse-video highlight is now a 59-column stub instead of a full-width bar. On a wide terminal a short bar is harder to catch with the eye.

3 · Option B — label rail, trailing spacer recommended

Keep expand=True, drop ratio=1, pin the label column to a fixed rail (18 for the grid, 20 for accounts) and add one empty trailing column that absorbs the slack instead. The numbers pack left exactly as in A, and because the table still spans the pane the cursor bar keeps its full width.
Why this one: it is the only option that gives you the tight coupling you asked for without paying for it somewhere else. The rail also means the numbers stop moving when you expand a category with a long child name — today every column shifts.
The cost: a label longer than the rail is truncated rather than widening the table. At 18, ▶ SUSCRIPCIONES (15) still fits with room to spare; the rail is a number you can pick.

4 · Option C — capped screen

Leave both tables alone and cap the screen: a max-width container (66 columns for the grid, 38 for the accounts band) that the tables expand inside. Tight on your wide terminal, and on a narrow one it behaves exactly as today because the cap never binds.
The cost: two caps to keep in step rather than one rule, and the grid keeps a few columns of padding by construction (the label column still absorbs whatever the cap leaves). The cursor bar spans the cap, which is a middle answer between A and B.

6 · What I need from you

Pick A, B or C — or say “B but rail at 24”, that kind of note is the useful kind. Two details worth deciding with it:
· Does the Accounts band follow the same rule as the grid? Drawn that way above, but the band is three columns and the grid is five, so they can differ.
· The band goes full-width because you have no People. The layout is a left/right split and the right half is empty, so Accounts takes the whole row. Capping the band fixes the symptom either way — worth knowing the cause is separate.
