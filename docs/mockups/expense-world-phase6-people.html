expense world — phase 6.2 sketch · people · 2026-08-16
This is the second and last additive piece of the 2026-08 engine rework, and the oddest one: the app has had a People section for months. It is on the dashboard, it is in expense world, it feeds the “owed” figure in the home banner, and it has unit tests. It has also been permanently empty, because until 2026-08-14 no endpoint anywhere could mark an account as a person. You have been looking at a shipped, tested, working section that could never contain anything.

What you cannot do today: record that you lent Eliana 200 soles. There is no way to add a person, so there is nothing to record it against, so the whole debt half of the product does not exist for you.

What the engine now does: a person can be created. A person is a bank account with a flag — money lent or borrowed is an ordinary transaction against her, and her balance is the debt: positive means she owes you, negative means you owe her. Everything after creation — rename, recolor, archive, delete — is already an accounts command and already works on people. Only the front door was missing.

Five things need your pick, marked in amber below: what you type to add a person, where archived people appear, and three questions about what happens to a person once the debt is settled and her balance reads zero. Nothing gets built until you choose.

2 · what the engine opened — one door, and only one
the shape of the new capability
after
creating a person is the only thing that needed a new route. everything else about a person is already an accounts command, and already works today: rename / recolor expense accounts update <id> --name "Eliana R." archive / unarchive expense accounts archive <id> delete / restore expense accounts delete <id> --yes look at one expense accounts get <id> list them expense accounts list --include-people There will never be an `expense people list` or `expense people edit`. Not "later" — never. The engine has exactly one people route and has stated it will not add more, because "is this a person" is only settable at the moment of creation. A second set of commands would be a copy of the accounts commands that already work.
drawnThis matters for decision 1 below: whichever name you pick has to survive the fact that it will always be a lonely command with no siblings.
3 · decision 1 of 5 — what do you type to add a person?
All three do exactly the same thing and take exactly the same information: a name, a currency, optionally a colour. The question is only what it reads like in your hands, and what each one implies about commands that do not exist.
A — expense people create
option
$ expense people create \ --name "Eliana" --currency-code PEN Created: 8f2c1a44-… name: Eliana currency_code: PEN is_person: True current_balance_cents: 0.00 $ expense people list Error: No such command 'list'.
drawnReads the most naturally, and is what you would guess first. The cost is in the second pane: a group called people promises a set of people commands, and there is only ever one. Every time you reach for people list it will look like a missing feature rather than a deliberate absence.
B — expense accounts create-person
chosen
$ expense accounts create-person \ --name "Eliana" --currency-code PEN Created: 8f2c1a44-… name: Eliana currency_code: PEN is_person: True current_balance_cents: 0.00 $ expense accounts list --include-people …lists her, alongside your bank accounts.
drawnLonger to type, and tells the truth: a person lives in accounts, which is where you will rename, archive and delete her anyway. Tab-completion puts create and create-person next to each other, so the choice is visible at the moment you make it.
C — expense accounts create --person
option
$ expense accounts create --person \ --name "Eliana" --currency-code PEN Created: 8f2c1a44-… name: Eliana currency_code: PEN is_person: True current_balance_cents: 0.00 $ expense accounts create --person \ --name "X" --sort-order 3 …the slot 3 means something different now.
drawnShortest, and the only one that hides something: the flag silently sends the request to a different endpoint with different rules — people are numbered in their own sequence, and an opening balance is refused. One command that quietly behaves two ways is the thing this repo has removed everywhere else.
4 · decision 2 of 5 — where do archived people appear?
Archiving is how you finish with someone. She leaves the People list, and the engine refuses to record anything else against her — that is the point of it. She is still visible on demand, with --include-archived. The engine hands the app archived people as their own separate list, deliberately kept apart from archived bank accounts (its words: archived Eliana should not sit among your archived cards). So the only question is where the second panel goes.
D — at the bottom, after archived accounts

5 · the question behind decisions 3 and 4 — what is a settled person?
why zero is not nothing
drawn
Eliana pays you back. Her balance becomes 0.00. Does she disappear from the People list? The engine will not decide this for you, and it refused to. Dropping a person the moment her balance hits zero was proposed and rejected, for three reasons worth repeating because they are all things you would eventually hit: 1 "she paid me back" and "I never wrote the loan down" would look identical on screen. 2 people would flicker in and out of the list as rows land — she vanishes the instant the repayment is recorded, reappears the next time you lend her anything. 3 a coincidental zero is not nothing: lent 200, borrowed 200 is two live debts that happen to cancel, and hiding it would erase both. So a settled person always comes back from the engine. What the app does with her is a display choice, and it is yours. The suggested shape is to fold them rather than drop them: People Eliana S/ 200 Marco −S/ 450 ▸ 3 settled Two surfaces need an answer, and they can legitimately answer differently — one is a printed page that cannot be unfolded, the other is a screen where you can press a key.
drawnThe definition used throughout: settled means the balance is exactly zero in her own currency. Not "roughly zero", and not zero after conversion — a person whose currency has no exchange rate today must never be mistaken for someone who paid you back.

8 · decision 5 of 5 — how do you add a person inside expense world?
On the Accounts screen, n opens the New Account form today: name, currency, colour, then ctrl+s. A person needs the identical three fields — the only difference is which door the app knocks on when you save. Three ways to make that difference visible.
L — a TYPE field on the same form
chosen
Manage ▸ Accounts ▸ New type bank name — * currency — * color — (optional) TYPE ▸ bank person ↑↓ pick · enter next · ctrl+s save
drawnOne key to remember (n), and the choice is the first thing you see, in the same list-picker the currency field already uses. The cost: one extra keypress every single time you add an ordinary bank account, forever, to answer a question that is nearly always "bank".
M — its own key
option
Manage ▸ Accounts Name Type Cur Balance BCP Soles bank PEN 1,250.00 Eliana person PEN 200.00 n new account p new person e edit a archive …press p: Manage ▸ People ▸ New name — * currency — * color — (optional)
drawnAdding a bank account is untouched, and the breadcrumb tells you which one you are in. Two keys to know instead of one, and it spends a letter on a screen that already uses n, e and a.
N — n asks first
option
Manage ▸ Accounts ▸ New What are you adding? ▸ bank account person ↑↓ pick · enter · esc cancel …then the ordinary form.
drawnUnmistakable — the two things are named in full before anything else happens. It is also a whole extra screen in front of the most common action on the page, and it is the only option here that introduces a new kind of widget rather than reusing one.
9 · stated, not asked
· The home banner does not change. It shows one number for people — owed — and it already drops that number entirely when everything is square. A settled person contributes zero to it, so there is nothing to fold there. No People list is being added to the home screen in this phase.

· Archived people already appear in expense world ▸ Accounts. That screen asks for people and archived rows together, and shows a Type column and a Status column, so an archived person will simply be there the moment one exists — dimmed, marked archived. Nothing to build; the pane below is the app's real renderer, given a person and an archived person:

real (rich)Printed by accounts.account_rows as it exists on main today. Editing, archiving and deleting a person all go through the ordinary account commands and already work — no new code on this screen at all.
· Two refusals you will meet, and both are correct. A person cannot be given an opening balance — a debt is built from the rows you record, not seeded — and a person's name collides with a bank account's name in the same currency, because the app keeps one name list, not two. A person called "Eliana" in soles and an account called "Eliana" in soles cannot both exist. The app will explain the second one when it happens rather than letting the raw error through.

· How this gets verified. Unit tests cannot see this feature at all — they use a recorded, fake engine, and a fake engine cannot grow a new endpoint. Neither can the drift script, which only notices fields the engine has stopped sending. So 6.2 is gated on the live contract suite against the disposable test engine on port 8001: create a person, lend to her, settle her, archive her, and confirm she moves from People to Archived people — with the real engine, on a throwaway database that is not your ledger.
Picked 2026-08-16: B + D + G + J + L, with L defaulted to bank.

1 · B — expense accounts create-person. Delegated to the implementer: the owner works in expense world, not in typed commands, and asked only that the name make sense. A was rejected because a group called people promises a set of people commands and there is exactly one, forever — every reach for people list would read as a missing feature. C was rejected because one command that silently posts to two different endpoints, with different numbering and a different opening-balance rule, is the pattern this repo has removed everywhere else.

2 · D — archived people at the bottom, after archived accounts. One flag, one block of archived things, clear of the month you read every morning. E was rejected because it splits the two archive panels apart and puts one idea in two distant places.

3 · G — ▸ 3 settled, a count and nothing else. Owner's words: a settled person "disappears from the People list until the balance changes — it's just visual, we didn't delete or archive the account." The count line is what keeps that honest. F was rejected because after a year of small loans the two people you actually owe are buried among a dozen zeros. H was rejected because the names line grows with the count and eventually needs its own truncation, reintroducing the same hiding by another door.

4 · J — a real fold in expense world, closed by default, opening on →/enter with the same caret as the Categories tree below it. I was rejected because a line that looks foldable and is not reads as broken on a screen that already folds; K because this panel sits above the month's spending and every settled person pushes it further down.

5 · L — a TYPE field on the same New Account form, prefilled to bank. The default is what answers the objection drawn in L's own pane: with bank already filled in, adding an ordinary account is still n then enter — the extra field costs a keypress you were pressing anyway. One key, one form, one place to look. M was rejected as a second key to remember on a screen that already uses n/e/a; N because it puts a whole extra screen, and a new kind of widget, in front of the most common action on the page.

Consequence carried into the edit form: whether a row is a person is settable only at creation, so TYPE is locked read-only when editing — the same treatment currency already gets.
