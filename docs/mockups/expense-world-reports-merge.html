§7 · What the merge decides no matter which option wins

  1. The balances are always "today", even when you page back. pgdn slides the grid to 2026-01 → 2026-04; the top band still shows today's 5,018.93. There is no historical-balance endpoint — GET /dashboard is current-month only. Options: (a) label the band "as of today" and leave it fixed (drawn above), (b) dim the band while the window is not the current month, (c) ask the engine for balances-as-of-date engine work.
  2. Two fetches, one screen. fetch() would call fetch_dashboard and fetch_range in the same worker — the first screen in the app to do that. Fine (both are already shared fetch_* helpers, and ActivityScreen already opens one client for a whole page), but it doubles first-paint latency, and it raises: if the dashboard call fails and the range call succeeds, does the screen error out or draw half?
  3. Option B's strip needs a number nobody returns. GET /dashboard gives per-account home cents and a totals block that is flow (inflow/outflow/net), not stock — there is no "sum of your accounts". Home's owed already sums people client-side (home.py:112), so the precedent exists, but that same code silently shrinks when a person is unpriced. Repeating it for accounts is a call to make deliberately, not by accident.
  4. The menu loses a row. Reports goes from two entries to one. Name? Overview (drawn), Report, Money, or keep Monthly report and just grow it. _SCREENS in home.py and its lockstep test move with it.
  5. Outstanding's category tree gets deleted, not moved. CategoriesView in outstanding.py becomes dead code the moment the grid's 4th column is on the same screen — along with _cat_is_shown / _shown_breakdown, which MonthGridView already has equivalents of. Roughly 120 lines out.
  6. Archived accounts stay invisible. GET /dashboard returns archived_accounts / archived_people and neither screen draws them today. The merge does not change that — flagging it only so the choice stays deliberate.
  7. Your ledger has no people. All six accounts are is_person: false. The People panel will render (none) on your machine until you add one — which is an argument for B (the strip just says people —) or for A drawing the People panel only when there are people, the way outstanding.py:301 already does.

§10 · Is C actually possible? — built, run, and screenshotted

2026-08-29, third pass. Yes. Everything below is a real Textual capture of a throwaway prototype fed your real ledger JSON — not a drawing. The probe lived outside the repo ($CLAUDE_JOB_DIR/tmp/proto_c.py) and is gone; no screen code was written.

The layout needs no new machinery. SectionScreen._show mounts whatever build() returns into Vertical(#card) — and a Horizontal holding a fixed-width #rail plus a width: 1fr #main is just another widget in that list. height: auto on the split resolves correctly, the Rich grid takes the leftover width and re-flows its columns on its own. The only screen-level change is CARD_WIDTH: it must become None (fill) instead of 64 / 92.

The doubt was well placed — but it's a number, not a blocker

rail = 30 cols · terminal 120 — what I first drew, and it does not fit

Your account names are long. At 30 columns BCP Signature USD becomes BCP Signature …, the Cur column collapses to a single …, and — worst — the balance itself truncates: 5,018…. A rail that hides money is not a rail. This is exactly the thing you were right to doubt, and it is why the sketch in §4 had to invent a $ marker to fit.

rail = 38 cols · terminal 120 — everything fits, nothing invented

38 columns is the answer. Full account names, the real Cur column, untruncated balances — and the grid still carries all four months including 467,189.17, the figure §4 predicted would break first. No $ marker, no shortened names, no home conversion. §4's caption said C "drops the Cur column to fit" — that was wrong; it only had to at 30.

rail = 38 · terminal 100 — the squeeze, and it degrades the right way

Twenty columns narrower, the rail is untouched (it is fixed) and the grid gives up category names first — SUSCRIPCIO… — while every amount stays whole. That is the best failure mode available: a truncated label is a guess you can make, a truncated number is a lie. Rich does this on its own; we do not have to write it.

The one thing that genuinely breaks — and the fix

defect — expand every category, scroll down: the rail leaves

#content is a VerticalScroll and the rail is inside it, so paging down scrolls the balances away too — leaving 38 columns of dead space for as long as you are scrolled. With every category expanded (drawn here) the grid is ~55 rows, so this is not an edge case, it is Tuesday. It would have shipped invisible: nobody notices it until the ledger is big enough to scroll, which yours is.

fixed — same scroll position, rail pinned

Two pages into a fully-expanded grid and the balances are still there. The fix is structural, not clever: the split moves out of #content and up to screen level, the rail gets height: 1fr, and only #main scrolls. Cost: this screen is the first to override SectionScreen.compose() rather than just build() — so its loading and error states need re-checking against the base class, which is real work but not novel work.

§11 · Option F — two panels, one above the other no engine work current direction

2026-08-29, fourth pass. C is retired — correctly: a fixed rail beside a table assumes horizontal room a terminal does not reliably have, and it is a shape that belongs to the web dashboard and the iOS client, where width is cheap and the window is yours. In a TUI, vertical is the axis you can spend, because the terminal scrolls down and never sideways.

So: Accounts panel, People panel under it, the four-month grid under that. All three full width, all three inside the normal Vertical(#card) — which means no compose() override, no pinning, none of §10's structural work. Captures below are real Textual runs on your ledger.

The one thing that decides whether it fits

boxed panels · 120 × 30 — the band eats the screen

Drawn the way Outstanding draws them today — box.SIMPLE with a column header — the band costs 20 lines: each panel spends a blank pad line top and bottom, plus a header row and its rule. At 30 lines you get two category rows. At 40 lines (not shown) it still clips net — the single most important number on the screen. This is the version that would have shipped if nobody measured.

lean panels · 120 × 30 — same window, all ten categories
